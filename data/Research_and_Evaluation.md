# CyberBondhu | Content curation, data and evaluation
CSE299 · Fall 2026 · Mahina Rahman · 25 November 2026

## 1. Scope and status
This contribution develops an educational, bilingual safety-game content bank for older adults in Bangladesh. The current JavaScript requests five scenarios from /api/game/start?lang=en&limit=5 and submits scenarioId and choiceId to /api/game/submit?lang=en. The response is expected to supply safe, feedback and correctChoiceText. The report proposes bilingual support; the present frontend hard-codes English. The delivered JSON provides 15 training scenarios and 10 separate assessment scenarios, with 75 bilingual choices and feedback entries in total. No participant study has yet been conducted.

## 2. Focused literature review
A targeted search of public publisher, university and research repository records was conducted on 25 November 2026 using terms including older adults phishing training, scam detection game, simulated inbox and behavioral evaluation. Five publications were selected for relevance to scenario design or training measurement. This is a focused abstract-level review, not a systematic review or a claim of exhaustive coverage.

[1] Davies et al. (2020) presented Fraud Bingo and a web-based scam-identification challenge, with an older-adult workshop involving 17 participants. Difficulties using the online challenge highlighted the need for age-appropriate interaction. CyberBondhu should use a single message per screen, large buttons and explanatory feedback. This small workshop does not establish general effectiveness.

[2] Chung and Yeung (2023) compared anti-scam board-game education with a comparison board game in 109 older participants. Self-reported prevention efficacy and scam awareness improved, including at two weeks. This supports accessible practice and follow-up measurement, but self-report is not direct evidence of fewer real fraud incidents.

[3] Sabo et al. (2023) compared phishing interventions with and without game features and with no intervention. Both training variants improved detection, but adding game features did not produce a meaningful additional benefit. CyberBondhu therefore emphasizes useful feedback and realistic decisions rather than points, rewards or competition. The study is supporting evidence, not a senior-specific trial of this application.

[4] Kazamia et al. (2024) evaluated Phish&Tips, a simulated inbox, with 37 participants. Improvements in reported confidence and understanding were not mirrored in recorded platform performance. This motivates objective pre/post decision scores, distinct unseen items and separate confidence measurement.

[5] Spot the Scam (2026) compared no intervention, reading and interactive activities across younger and older adults. The interventions increased caution but did not improve discrimination sensitivity. The game must include benign messages and measure overreaction, rather than rewarding the belief that all messages are fraudulent.

## 3. Design guidelines
Use short realistic messages and ask what the user should do next. Provide three clearly distinguished choices, exactly one safest action, and immediate nonjudgmental feedback explaining the warning cue and the safer alternative. Rotate the position of the correct answer. Include legitimate examples and practice independent verification, protection of PINs/OTPs, and avoidance of unexpected payments or links. Use ordinary Bangla and English with equivalent meaning, large high-contrast text, no timer, no penalty for mistakes, and accessible keyboard interaction. Do not display real contact details or active URLs. These are evidence-informed design decisions, not a proven optimal question format. Bilingual wording has been drafted and checked for semantic consistency; independent native-speaker and faculty review is still recommended.

## 4. Dataset curation and provenance
The financial scam dataset [6] contains English and Bangla scam and ham messages, described as anonymized by its creators (CC BY 4.0). BangalaBarta [7] provides 2,772 Bangla SMS records across smishing, promotional and normal classes (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0). Their landing pages were verified but raw file downloads were not accessible in this environment. No raw records from [6] or [7] are claimed as extracted. Instead, publicly visible examples from the multilingual Bengali SMS Smishing Dataset [8] and the UCI SMS Spam Collection [9] were inspected. The source preview for [8] does not independently establish whether every record is a real received message; check its underlying collection methodology and reuse terms before public release. The UCI spam example is treated as a suspicious offer, not proven phishing. 

Curation procedure: select relevant labelled examples; manually screen for an actionable risk or benign control; remove numbers, links, account identifiers and names where applicable; paraphrase instead of reproducing raw text; translate into the other language; construct safe and unsafe actions; review feedback; retain source URL, source label and preview locator. The locators in Dataset_Sources.csv are search-preview line references, not original corpus IDs; use the adapted message/category to locate the original and assign stable row IDs when raw access is available. The delivered bank contains 13 adaptations from [8], one UCI adaptation and one authored benign control. The assessment bank contains 10 authored items. No live links appear in game message text. These are educational adaptations, not an independently authenticated real-world corpus. The dataset-mining requirement is therefore partially fulfilled; the original dataset files must still be downloaded and verified for a full provenance audit.

## 5. Evaluation methodology
Research question: Does three rounds of bilingual scenario-based practice improve older adults’ ability to select safe actions on new messages? Recruit approximately 15–30 volunteers aged 60+ as a feasibility target, subject to faculty approval and consent. Use participant codes, age bands and preferred language; collect no passwords, phone numbers or financial information. Participation is voluntary and withdrawal is allowed. Offer breaks and record assistance rather than silently coaching responses.

Procedure: administer five pre-test items with no feedback; deliver three rounds of five distinct training items with immediate feedback; administer five matched but unseen post-test items without feedback; then collect a short usability and confidence questionnaire. Counterbalance pre/post forms if practical (IDs 101–105 and 106–110) and keep the assigned language consistent. Randomize item/choice ordering without changing IDs. Do not expose held-out questions during practice. An optional delayed test at 7–14 days requires a separate unseen item set, not provided here. A waitlist or reading-only comparison group would strengthen causal inference; without it, report within-participant changes as preliminary rather than causal.

Primary metric: safe-action accuracy = correct choices / attempted items × 100. Learning gain = post-test accuracy minus pre-test accuracy in percentage points. Secondary: training round accuracy; unsafe-choice rate on suspicious items = unsafe choices / suspicious items attempted; benign overreaction rate = choices that unnecessarily report or block benign messages / benign items attempted (do not count unrelated credential-sharing options as overreaction); completion rate = participants completing all phases / enrolled participants; assistance rate = participants needing help / participants tested. If a separate scam/not-scam question is implemented, calculate scam sensitivity and benign false-positive rate from that classification; action choices alone do not measure detection sensitivity. Record confidence (1–5) separately. Use the optional classification column only if implemented.

Analysis: report counts, mean and median paired change, standard deviation and a paired confidence interval where feasible. A paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test or exact sign test can be considered for a small pilot, with limitations from five-item score granularity and tied scores. Report missing responses and attrition rather than imputing success. An illustrative target is +20 percentage points mean gain with no increase in benign overreaction; this is a proposed project criterion, not a validated benchmark or measured result. A single-group pilot cannot establish real-world fraud reduction. With just one benign item per test, the overreaction estimate is coarse; expand the test bank for a stronger study.

Usability questionnaire (1 = strongly disagree; 5 = strongly agree): I could read the messages easily; I understood the answer choices; I understood the explanations; I could navigate without help; I feel confident checking suspicious messages. Report individual item distributions; this custom survey is not the validated System Usability Scale. Add an optional open-ended question about confusing language or controls.
